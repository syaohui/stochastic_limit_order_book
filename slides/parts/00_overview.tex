\begin{frame}{What this deck covers}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \small
      \begin{enumerate}
        \setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
        \item \textbf{The problem.} What a limit order book is, and what a liquidity provider (LP) fights against.
        \item \textbf{The model toolkit.} Queueing, Hawkes, and propagator models, each stated with its key result.
        \item \textbf{Order flow.} How imbalance at one level and at several levels moves the price.
        \item \textbf{The LP's problems now.} Markouts, queue priority, latency, quoting with signals, and liquidity in automated market makers (AMMs).
        \item \textbf{The frontier, 2023 to 2026.} Generative and foundation models, learning to make markets, rough volatility.
        \item \textbf{Open problems.}
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.44\textwidth}
      \begin{alertblock}{How to read the numbers}
        Every figure and number comes from open code in this repository. Each closed-form result is checked against an independent simulation, and every estimate carries a standard error.
      \end{alertblock}
      \vspace{0.4em}
      {\small All rate parameters are illustrative. Nothing here is fitted to market data or meant as trading advice.}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}
